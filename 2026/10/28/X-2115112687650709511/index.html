How to give Claude a designer's taste and build your own landing page

发表于2026-10-28|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @X（X） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

A designer skill gets you a landing page that looks like a designer made it, in an afternoon, on your normal Claude plan.

Anthropic built skills so Claude can carry a specialist’s rules into any chat. A skill is a folder with one main file, SKILL.md. Claude reads its name and description up front and pulls in the full instructions only when a task needs them.

I’ve packed 20+ specialists into my own Claude skills bundle. If you sell anything online, a designer skill is the one I’d build first, because a landing page is where a vague prompt costs you the most money.

Below is how to build one from scratch with Claude, the five prompts that take you from an empty chat to a live link, and the check that makes the page work on your phone.

Our designer skill is a rulebook Claude reads before it builds. You give it the brief, Claude builds against your rules, and it keeps fixing the page until it passes your checklist.

What goes inside a designer skill

Every good designer skill has two halves and a checklist. The first half decides what the page says and how it’s laid out. The second half decides how it looks.

As you can imagine, the checklist decides when the page is done.

The strategy half starts with an intake. Before writing a word, Claude asks for the one action you want visitors to take, the offer, the buyer, their top three objections, where your traffic comes from and what proof you have.

Then it fixes the structure. Every page gets the same 12 sections in the same order, from a headline that names the outcome and the audience down to an FAQ, a guarantee and a final button that matches the first one.

The strategy half also sets the copy rules. One offer, one audience, one button, and real numbers in place of words like “streamline”. “Save time” becomes “cut your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes”.

The visual half is where your taste lives. It names your one font, your colors as hex codes, a short list of allowed spacing values, your corner style and one motion curve for every animation. Fixed values stop Claude from guessing a new look every time you ask.

The checklist closes it out. Strategy, visuals and shipping basics like a 404 page, form validation and image alt text all get a tick before the page counts as done.

You don’t write the file yourself.

Instead, Claude interviews you about your brand and writes it.

If you don’t have brand colors yet, tell Claude so. Ask it to propose three palettes based on the 3 sites you named, render each one as a small sample, and pick the one that feels like you.

Save the file as SKILL.md. In Claude, create a Project, add SKILL.md to the project’s knowledge and start a chat inside it. Every chat in that project now follows your rules. If you use Claude Code, put the file in a folder called landing-page-design inside your project’s .claude/skills folder, and Claude loads it whenever you ask for a page.

Build one skill for one job. A landing page skill that does one thing well beats a giant brand file that also tries to cover your emails, decks and posts, because when a page comes out wrong you know which rule to fix.

In practice, the difference looks like this:

Weak offer line: “My coaching business.”

Strong offer line: “A 6-week LinkedIn coaching program for freelance designers who want 2 new clients a month. The one action is booking a free 20-minute call.”

The second one answers half the intake before Claude asks a single question.

Give the rest of the intake the same care. For objections, write the three reasons people hesitate in their own words, like “I tried a coach before and it didn’t work”. For proof, give one number or quote you can back up. For traffic, say where visitors come from, so the headline can match the post or ad they just clicked.

Start with one page for your best offer and one button.

Within a week you’ll know whether people click, and one page is quick to fix when they don’t.

Improve one section at a time

Ask for changes one section at a time, in a fixed order.

Never ask Claude to rebuild the whole page to change one part or you’ll spend days going back and forth with it.

Working one section at a time keeps every change small enough to check. It’s more accurate and less time consuming.

For the hero, give Claude three headline formulas to work from.

“{Outcome} without {pain}”, “The {category} for {audience}” and “Get {result} in {time}”. Ask for one option from each and pick the one that sounds like you.

Add the browser check before you ship

A page can look finished in the chat and still break on a phone. A button wraps onto two lines, the headline leaves one word alone on its last line and the signup form accepts an empty email field.

Claude Code can open the page in a real browser at phone, tablet and desktop width, screenshot each one, then look at its own screenshots and fix what it sees.

The checklist takes more thought than the build.

Claude writes a hero section in a minute but knowing the page works for a stranger on an old phone takes a list you wrote before the build started, which is why the checklist lives at the bottom of your skill.

Put the page online

Getting the page live takes about five minutes. Rename the downloaded file to index.html and put it in a folder on its own. Open Netlify Drop, log in and drag the folder onto the page. Netlify publishes it and gives you a free live link you can share right away. To update the page later, drag the new folder onto the same site’s deploys.

Before you share the link, run the shipping part of your checklist. Privacy and terms links in the footer, a custom 404 page, email validation on the form, a favicon, a page title and meta description, a social sharing image and alt text on every image.

Decide on search too. A page for a time-limited ad campaign should carry a noindex tag so Google skips it. A page for an offer you’ll sell for years should be indexed, with a clear title, a meta description and the FAQ written as plain questions and answers.

What your job becomes

You started with a brief and now you’ve ended with a fully fledged landing page.

Moving forward, you’ll spend time on the decisions that need taste, you choose the offer, set the look and you approve or reject the final screenshots.

That review takes about ten minutes, and it decides whether the page sells.

Claude handles the spacing, the type sizes and the admin work.

You handle the brief, the brand and the final yes.

Write your rulebook once, and every page Claude builds follows it.

Your first landing page can be live by Friday.

LLMs don’t think, you do.
