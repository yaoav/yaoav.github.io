Nvidia Q3 Earnings Report: Reading Between the Lines

发表于2026-11-19|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @blocmates（blocmates.） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Nvidia just dropped its Q3 report. Unfortunately, there are no more affairs to report about, but there are some fascinating details in there that we promise are worth your attention.

Let’s jump right in.

The headlines:

  • Revenue has jumped from $96.2 billion in Q2 to $115.8 billion in Q3. A big 20% jump
  • Data centers now account for 93% of Nvidia’s revenue. That’s $107.6 billion coming in from their data center business
  • Shareholder returns: $28.1 billion through buybacks and dividends
  • Remaining buyback authorization: $227.3 billion
  • Operating income: $77.5 billion, up 115% YoY
  • Gross margin is under pressure. Reported at 74.8% for Q3 (down 0.2% QoQ) and expected to drop to 71-72% in Q4

Beyond the headline numbers, there are two important takeaways from the Q3 financials.

Pivot to infrastructure

As the revenue suggests, Nvidia is transitioning from simply a GPU company to a full-stack AI infrastructure company.

The company’s future is increasingly tied to:

  • Hyperscaler capital expenditure
  • Enterprise AI adoption
  • Inference demand
  • AI agent deployment
  • Sovereign AI infrastructure

This pivot is also clear with some of the partnerships announced in the report:

  • Vera CPUs: custom CPUs for AI infrastructure.
  • NVLink Fusion: connecting NVIDIA infrastructure to custom XPUs.
  • Spectrum-X: networking infrastructure.
  • BlueField-4: data processing and infrastructure security.
  • DSX POD: complete AI factory reference designs.
  • OpenShell: infrastructure for AI agent execution and security.

On top of this, Nvidia’s cloud service agreement and data center lease commitments have increased from $74 billion to $102 billion, further emphasizing the commitment to being at the heart of the physical infrastructure of AI.

The CFO’s commentary also disclosed a $105 billion guarantee connected to a huge OpenAI infrastructure development in Ohio.

The $390 billion bet on memory

AI GPUs are heavily dependent on high-bandwidth memory (HBM), which is expensive and difficult to manufacture at scale.

At the same time, Nvidia has some massive projects that are just beginning, like Blackwell and Vera Rubin.

So Nvidia has invested $390 billion in a multi-year procurement commitment for this memory to ensure it stays ahead of the pack.

The main risk here is simply a decrease in demand for AI infrastructure and memory, leaving them with big unnecessary bills, but other than that, Nvidia is making its plans clear.
