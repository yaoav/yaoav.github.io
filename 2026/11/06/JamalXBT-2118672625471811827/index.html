How to Build Your Own AI Content Factory With Claude + Picsart MCP (Full Commands)

one plugin, eight commands, micro dramas, AI influencers, anime and ads from one chat, a cast that stays locked for the whole season, a price on every shot before you pay, and where the money sits

One ReelShort series reportedly cost about $200,000 and a week on set.

Actors, a crew, a vertical camera rig, a location for every cliffhanger. Then the app sold those episodes a minute at a time and pulled an estimated $1.2 billion in in-app revenue in 2025.

In Claude Code, the same kind of series starts with one line:

I’ve watched a lot of AI video tools hand you a render and a bill. This one hands you a questionnaire first.

Claude starts with the questions a showrunner asks: how many episodes, how long, which language, does every episode hand off to the next one. Then it writes a series bible. Then it casts. Video comes last, and only after you’ve seen a price.

That’s the Picsart plugin for Claude Code, and micro dramas are only its flagship. The same factory turns out AI influencer posts, anime shorts, motion ads, reels and tutorials. 42 skills, eight commands, MIT license, and a rulebook that reads like it was written by someone who has burned real money on AI video.

Below: the install, the commands, the gates, the bible, the cast lock, the bill, and three ways people get paid for this.

The other 34 skills are the crew. The commands load them by name: cast design, shot generation, film cutting, finishing, retention hooks, spoken captions, voice-over, color grade, quality review.

You never call them yourself. Picsart’s README says to install the whole plugin for exactly that reason: one command reaches for a dozen others.

3/ THE GATES THAT PROTECT YOUR CREDITS

Every command reads one file before it does anything: picsart-video-rules. It sorts all work into five interactions. Three of them carry the plugin.

ASK - one decision at a time. Two to four options, the recommended one first, each saying what it changes.

The rules ban “looks good?” questions outright, because everyone answers yes. Claude asks about whatever is still open: which hook, which payoff, which style.

SPEND - a price, then a yes. Before any paid call Claude runs a cost estimate and reads your balance.

The rule is literal: “never quote a number from memory.” A batch of generations is one quote and one yes. The draft and the final get priced side by side.

REVIEW - a candidate until you accept it. A generated episode stays a draft until you sign off on it. Notes you give once (“always end on her face”) go into the project record with the reason and get applied to everything after.

And one line that sounds small: free work never asks permission. Outlines, boards and previews just happen.

The short drama command runs on those gates:

The sentence I’d tape to my monitor: “Decide on text and stills before buying video.”

That’s where AI video budgets usually die. People pay for motion while the story is still a draft, then pay again when the story changes.

4/ THE BIBLE COMES BEFORE THE FIRST PIXEL

This is the part that made me take the plugin seriously.

Claude reads your brief in full and asks only what’s missing: format (serial, pilot, standalone, finale or sitcom), episode count, episode length, language, whether each episode has a successor, a template or freeform. It’s told to never assume an episode count.

Then it writes the bible. The fields come straight from the drama-series-writing skill:

  • emotional promise - the feeling the viewer comes back for, shown at least once in every episode
  • season question - one question the finale answers, and what the answer costs
  • characters - a want, a flaw, a secret, a competence, and the obsession that causes a bad choice
  • power graph - desire, trust, debt, shared history and risk for every main pair
  • reveal bank - hidden facts, where they’re planted, who is allowed to learn them
  • two plots - a deadline outside, a secret inside
  • recurring sets - four to six places, three or four supporting speakers

My favorite note in the file: give the antagonist a want they could state reasonably aloud.

The brief I’d start with:

The bible gets locked before the season arc, and every episode after that reads from it.

The skill opens with a line every screenwriter should steal: “A series keeps its promise through changed consequences.” Each episode leaves the characters somewhere they can’t reset.

That sentence is what keeps someone paying for episode 11.

5/ ONE CAST, LOCKED FOR THE WHOLE SEASON

AI series used to die on face drift. Episode three, and the lead has a different jaw.

The plugin handles it the way a production company would. It designs the whole series inventory once, people, sets and props, under one quote. Each character gets a turnaround sheet. You approve the tiles, they get locked, and every later shot is built from the locked pictures.

An approved key frame is the contract: every later shot of that character starts from it.

Files land in a project tree Claude creates in your Picsart Drive:

Come back a week later with “the next episode” and Claude reads the series record, lists what’s locked in the Library, tells you where the series stands and picks up at the next episode. The Library is the authority on who is locked at which version.

The look works the same way. The film setup opens once per series, the frame defaults to 9:16, and every later episode inherits the lock.

6/ WHAT RUNS UNDER THE HOOD

Model choice gets the same treatment as everything else. The skill offers the options, validates the parameters and prices the run before anything generates.

The catalog behind the connector covers image, video and audio: Kling, Veo 3.1, Runway Gen 4.5, Seedance 2.5, Sora 2 Pro, GPT Image, Flux, Recraft, Nano Banana Pro, and ElevenLabs for voices and music. Kling 4.0 landed in Picsart in October with 30-second and 4K video generation.

Picsart showed the bigger picture with its own demo: Claude Fable 5.1 building a 3D scene in Blender over MCP while Picsart MCP handled the texturing and the audio. All of it ran in one chat.

7/ NO TERMINAL? THE SAME STUDIO HAS NAMES

Half your team will never open Claude Code. Picsart runs the same idea inside its app as a roster of agents, 23 live right now, each with a job title:

  • Aura - creative director, the agent that runs every agent
  • Saga - showrunner and screenwriter, vertical drama series with consistent casts
  • Lina - film producer and director, 8 to 12 scenes planned before generation, original score, delivered to your Drive
  • Ren - anime studio, live since October 29
  • Nora - localization designer and video dubber, one campaign adapted per market
  • Kai - content repurposer, one video into two weeks of content

Ren is the one I’d test first.

Picsart’s own demo shows why: someone uploads a photo of a pet rooster named Jalu and types one line, “Make a 30 second fight beat with my pet Jalu as the lead character.” They ask for a 3x3 storyboard, Ren draws it, and Jalu comes back as an anime fighter on rainy temple steps in a bamboo forest.

Bring him an idea, a photo of yourself or your own clip, and he turns it into original anime: a 5-30 second vertical clip, an episode with a script and shot list, or a series with locked characters.

Every character gets a turnaround sheet before a single shot, and he only makes original looks, so you won’t get a knockoff of a famous show.

9/ WHERE THE MONEY IS

Picsart’s own challenges. Picsart runs creator challenges back to back.

**In September and October alone: **

MiniMax H3 with $50,000 for five winners, Nano Banana 2.1 Spooky with $25,000, Seedance Nightmare with $25,000, and the Kling 4.0 Microdrama Challenge with $50,000 split as $10,000 for each of five winners.

That’s $150,000 in prize pools in two months, and the last one asked for exactly what /picsart:make-short-drama produces.

Series for the platforms. Omdia puts micro drama revenue at roughly $14 billion in 2026, up from about $11 billion in 2025. The apps live on volume. A three-episode pilot with a locked bible and a locked cast is a pitch you can actually send.

The same series also travels. Picsart Flow added a video translator with lip-sync in October, and Nora adapts a campaign per market, so one season becomes five language versions without a re-shoot.

Branded micro dramas. A brand that would never fund a live-action series will happily pay for three episodes where its product drives the plot. The skill set covers every stage of that job: bible, cast, shots, captions, music, delivery in 9:16.

And if you work inside a marketing team, the person who can ship a branded pilot by Friday is the one who gets the raise.
