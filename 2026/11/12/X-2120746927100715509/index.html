Who Is Really Behind the Hunt for Jensen Huang?

发表于2026-11-12|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @X（X） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

“Doth our law judge any man, before it hear him, and know what he doeth?” (John 7:51, KJV)

It is no secret that Jensen Huang and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei are locked in an open clash over AI policy and chip exports, from heated disagreements in Washington to Huang publicly rejecting Amodei’s push for sweeping export bans.

Yet look at who is suddenly piling on to drive Huang out. Anthropic’s people overlap with the think tanks, ex-officials and forums behind this coverage. Its money backs lawmakers in the congressional push. Its funding network helped build the think tank many of the critics come from.

One coincidence is a coincidence. A string of them isn’t.

When the people leading a pile-on against a CEO keep turning out to be connected to his loudest industry rival, shouldn’t those connections be disclosed? Anyone covering this story should be asking who stands to benefit. So far, almost no one is.

At first I thought it was just China hawks and the media teaming up in a midterm year to go after Nvidia and Jensen Huang. But the coverage kept getting stranger, and some of it was openly racist.

So I started pulling the threads. I began with @AnaSwanson, the New York Times reporter who broke the spy story, and asked why such a thin lead got so much play.

I read through her major reporting on AI and chips and looked at who keeps showing up as a source. Anyone who knows journalism knows you don’t keep going back to the same person, and keep adopting their framing, unless there’s real trust.

One name kept coming up: Jack Clark (@jackclarkSF), Anthropic co-founder and policy chief.

1. The reporters and their sources

Clark started out as a Bloomberg reporter, then became Policy Director at OpenAI. He knows how the press works from the inside.

In May of last year he gave the pre-dinner remarks, titled “Software Singularity,” at an Aspen Institute AI policy conference, a room full of people who work on chips and tech geopolitics.

Source: https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/FINAL-AI.Spain_Report_2025-1.pdf

2. The Democratic senator who piled on

After the Times story ran, @ChrisCoons came out swinging: “the insane relationship between the Trump Administration, Chinese spies, and AI billionaires… Congress will investigate.

In July, Coons received $2,500 from AnthroPAC, Anthropic’s corporate PAC. Jack Clark personally put $5,000 into that PAC.

Source: https://elections.transformernews.ai/pacs/C00946111

Clark lives and works in San Francisco. Coons represents Delaware. And it’s not a hometown tie: in his 2019 House Intelligence Committee testimony, Clark listed his education as “Varndean College, Brighton,” in the UK.

Source: https://docs.house.gov/meetings/IG/IG00/20190613/109620/HHRG-116-IG00-Bio-ClarkJ-20190613.pdf

Clark was also a non-resident fellow at Georgetown’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET). Remember that name.

3. The Georgetown / CSET circle

A striking number of the loudest voices against Huang come out of @Georgetown:

  • @MarkZJia, a Georgetown Law professor and former Supreme Court clerk
  • @RyanFedasiuk, a Georgetown adjunct and former CSET analyst, who publicly called for Huang to resign
  • @dennisw5, a former senior CIA official now teaching at Georgetown

CSET was set up in 2019 inside Georgetown’s School of Foreign Service to study the security side of AI and chips. It launched with a $55 million grant from Open Philanthropy, the biggest funder in the Effective Altruism world. Open Phil was co-founded by Holden Karnofsky, who later joined Anthropic.

Source: https://theinfoweb.org/organizations/open-philanthropy/

Look at Jia in particular. When the Pentagon labeled Anthropic a “supply-chain risk,” Lawfare cited his work on the legal limits of national-security designations.

Source: https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/pentagon's-anthropic-designation-won't-survive-first-contact-with-legal-system

So when Anthropic was the target, the legal bar for a “national-security risk” was supposed to be high. Why does that bar vanish when the target is Jensen Huang?

4. The Financial Times

@AsiaLens (Demetri Sevastopulo) of the FT pushed the “romantic relationship” angle about Huang and an alleged Chinese spy.

Source: https://www.ft.com/content/a55a2a22-4479-4f93-a445-6147a5c6d790

In September 2025, the FT broke the exclusive that Anthropic was cutting off Chinese-controlled companies from its AI.

Source: https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/anthropic-alibaba-dispute-puts-ai-distillation-under-spotlight-what-is-it-126062600540_1.html

Source: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions

Where do exclusives like that usually come from?

5. The spark on X

Then came the post that blew up X. On the night of Nov. 6, Chris McGuire (@ChrisRMcGuire) of the Council on Foreign Relations took the “romantic relationship” framing and pushed it further, writing that Huang was “sleeping with” a Chinese spy.

McGuire has about 12,000 followers. What most people missed is his background: he worked under Tarun Chhabra on Biden’s National Security Council, and Chhabra is now Anthropic’s Head of National Security Policy. In June, the two shared a stage at the DSET forum in Taipei.

Source: https://dset.tw/en/event-news-en/481-4/

Within hours, Rep. Seth Magaziner, Susan Rice, Adam Kinzinger and Nicholas Kristof were all quoting it. A 12K-follower account’s post climbed to more than 9 million views, the most-viewed post of the whole episode.

6. The Senate Banking Committee

In February, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei met with Senate Banking Committee members, pushing for tighter curbs on AI chips to China. Soon after, Sens. Warren and Banks announced a Senate version of the AI OVERWATCH Act.

Source: https://fgsglobal.com/insights/newsletters/ai-policy-newsletter/ai-policy-newsletter-february-13-2026

One of the committee’s senior Republicans, Sen. Kevin Cramer (@SenKevinCramer), took $1,000 from AnthroPAC in July. He has now publicly demanded that Huang step down as CEO.

Source: https://elections.transformernews.ai/pacs/C00946111

Source: https://www.roic.ai/news/senator-cramer-calls-for-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-to-resign-amid-spy-allegations-11-11-2026

7. The money, and Anthropic’s own board

This cycle, Anthropic runs two money machines:

  • AnthroPAC (FEC ID C00946111), which gives directly to campaigns.
  • $40 million to Public First Action, a 501(c)(4) group with affiliated super PACs. Amodei added $1 million of his own.

Public First Action has run ads praising lawmakers who back chip-export bills, including House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast, who also received $2,500 from AnthroPAC. Mast is one of the chairs behind the Nov. 4 letter demanding answers from Huang.

Source: https://publicfirstaction.us/news/public-first-action-launches-15-million-conservative-ai-consensus-project

Source: https://foreignaffairs.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairmen-mast-moolenaar-and-crawford-request-jensen-huang-brief-congress-on-link-to-chinese-intelligence-asset

Then there’s Anthropic’s governance. Richard Fontaine sits on Anthropic’s Long-Term Benefit Trust, the body that will pick a majority of its board. Fontaine is also CEO of the Center for a New American Security (CNAS), one of Washington’s main shops for chip export-control policy.

CNAS’s chip-smuggling work lays out the playbook now aimed at Nvidia: go after Southeast Asian shell companies and third-country middlemen. Fontaine has sat on Anthropic’s trust since June 2025.

Source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/national-security-expert-richard-fontaine-appointed-to-anthropic-s-long-term-benefit-trust

Source: https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/countering-ai-chip-smuggling-has-become-a-national-security-priority

The hawks and Anthropic aren’t just aligned. They share leadership.

One coincidence is a coincidence. A string of them isn’t.

The New York Times reporter who broke the Jensen Huang spy story? A longtime source of hers happens to be Anthropic’s co-founder.

The FT reporter who pushed the “romantic relationship” angle? He happened to land the exclusive on Anthropic cutting off Chinese-controlled companies.

The man who turned “romantic relationship” into “sleeping with a Chinese spy” on X? He happens to be a former subordinate of Anthropic’s head of national security policy.

The senator demanding Huang resign? He happens to sit on the committee Dario Amodei lobbied behind closed doors, and he took money from Anthropic’s PAC.

The House chair who co-signed the letter demanding answers from Huang? He happens to be backed by Anthropic’s PAC and by the group Anthropic funded with $40 million.

One coincidence is a coincidence. What do you call a string of them?

When The New York Times broke the story on Oct. 29 and The Wall Street Journal followed on Oct. 30, the Journal claimed Singapore officials were concerned about Alice Huang and had investigated her ties to China.

The very next day, Singapore’s Ministry of Home Affairs put out a formal written statement: “Neither the Singapore Government nor its officials said this. And there were no such investigations conducted in respect of Alice Huang.” It even published the Journal’s questions and the government’s reply, which never mentioned her.

A week later, as the story exploded around the world, the very same outlets all somehow, by sheer coincidence, failed to notice that official denial.

Source: www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/clarification-statement-on-wall-street-journals-article+

Five days in, with still no evidence, the Chinese-spy story was falling apart. And then, by another coincidence, it suddenly became a story about Jensen Huang having an affair.

The same New York Times, only this time with a whole new set of reporters on the byline. Perhaps that’s a coincidence too.

And by yet another coincidence, it all lands just as Anthropic launches a roughly $61 billion IPO, the second-largest in history, days after more than half a trillion dollars was wiped off Nvidia’s market value.

the last coincidence before the ipo

Anthropic just hire Washington bench,

from both administrations

  • Tarun Chhabra — Head of National Security Policy, Anthropic. Formerly Coordinator for Technology and National Security, National Security Council.
  • Ben Buchanan — now at Anthropic. Formerly White House AI adviser and an architect of the AI chip “diffusion rule.”
  • Elizabeth Kelly — now at Anthropic. Formerly Director of the U.S. AI Safety Institute.
  • Joel Meyer — leads Anthropic’s federal civilian public-sector work. Formerly a senior official at the Department of Homeland Security, where he set up its first AI task force.
  • Benjamin Della Rocca and Kristine Lee — now at Anthropic. Both formerly National Security Council staff.

From the Trump administration

  • Chris Liddell — Anthropic board member since February 2026. Formerly White House Deputy Chief of Staff (first term).
  • Sihao Huang — Head of Frontier Compute Strategy since September 2026. Formerly Senior Policy Advisor for AI, White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.
  • Sarah Heck — Head of Policy, leads Anthropic’s talks with the White House. Formerly National Security Council (first term).
  • Mary Croghan — Anthropic’s D.C. policy team. Formerly White House Office of Cabinet Affairs (first term).

The playbook

Oct 26: Anthropic files for its Nasdaq IPO.

Oct 29–30: NYT and WSJ raise intel concerns over Alice Huang’s ties to Nvidia.

Oct 31: Singapore’s MHA says it never investigated her. Late Oct–Nov 3: Anthropic’s IPO valuation is under pressure.

Nov 6: The FT NYT reports intel assessments and “intimate ties.” McGuire’s repost makes it go viral.

Nov 8: Jensen Huang admits to an affair with noname. Nov 10: Anthropic launches its ~$61B IPO roadshow.

Nov 11: The NYT reports a second affair.

Nov 12: Jensen Huang resigns. DOJ and FBI say there’s no conclusion on whether Alice Huang is a spy.

Remember these numbers:

**Anthropic: ** $40 million to Public First Action. ~$26 million in allied super PAC spending across 16 races. $15 million in ads naming 16 Republican lawmakers. $5.33 million from employees, reportedly $1 million from Amodei himself.

Nvidia’s : $263,000 raised. To candidates: nothing. Zero.
