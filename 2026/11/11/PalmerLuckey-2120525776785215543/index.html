Veteran Ready: Hiring for Careers of Conviction

发表于2026-11-11|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @PalmerLuckey（Palmer Luckey） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

I never intended to join the military. As a 18-year old kid, I showed up to the gates of the Citadel in Charleston, South Carolina, on a soccer scholarship, mostly interested in getting a free college education and the chance to make some friends playing sports.

Then, 9/11 happened. Much like many others in my generation, I felt a sense of duty in response. I immediately went to the Army ROTC department and signed a contract to serve after graduation. What followed was a decade in the military serving as an Army aviation officer, flying the AH-64D Apache in the 101st Airborne and the MH-47G Chinook in the 160th SOAR. It included heavy combat, such as serving as the air mission commander for the rescue force during at the Battle of Kamdesh at COP Keating — one of the deadliest days in the fight in Afghanistan. My service ultimately ended in 2015 after injury, and though my journey within the military was over, I didn’t expect that my time advancing the mission I signed up for was not.

That was before I heard about Anduril.

After departing service, I spent time in business school, investment banking, and working at a tech company. Every experience was worthwhile. But none could approximate the intense sense of purpose and camaraderie I felt in my service. Selling clicks to generate revenue was one thing. The opportunity to support those still in uniform would be another.

That’s when a former Citadel classmate had posted something online about this company he was working at — Anduril. I’d never heard of it. A small tech company at that time, it had something like two customers, and just a few products. I took a flier on a job, my wife and I sold our home, and moved to California where I became roughly employee number 200. Little did I know just how life-changing — and meaningful — that decision would be.

Nothing can replicate time serving, but it immediately felt like my contributions as a civilian had purpose. Instead of flying helicopters into battle, I was finding ways to push autonomous drones into the hands of the warfighter. Rather than transport operators to a combat zone, I was supporting them in real time: getting feedback on how our systems performed, what worked, and what needed improvement. It immediately felt like the next step in my personal mission: to defend the nation, while no longer wearing the uniform. To support service members by ensuring they had the best tech available.

That last part was especially important. I’ve flown hundreds of missions piloting exquisite systems carrying precision weapons and — far more importantly — my fellow soldiers. I know firsthand what it means when a system doesn’t perform the way you need it to in the moment of decision. I know just how much maintenance, measured in hours, sweat, and ingenuity, that military technicians put into making an aircraft fly. That’s why Anduril’s thesis — to build high-performing systems that can be put in harm’s way without putting our service members at risk — stood out to me from the very beginning.

Committing to it has paid me back, several times over. Today, we’re far from a 200-person company — we’re 12,000 and growing. Now working as the Chief Administrative Officer, I have the chance to learn from veterans like the ones that brought me on in the first place. They are interspersed throughout the company, working on the hardest problems in national security: going downrange with operators to get their realtime feedback, applying their technical expertise to designing new platforms for the military, or using their hard-won leadership skills to ensure that a company designed from the start to disrupt how the defense industry works still does. They are leading teams as our company grows and changes in ways few places in American business have in decades.

Anduril has a significant proportion of veterans in its workforce: 13 percent overall. The mission appeal is obvious. But plenty of technology and defense companies hire veterans. What separates Anduril isn’t just that we are veteran-friendly, but veteran ready.

It shows in how we work. Even on the most consequential projects, it’s not uncommon for teammates to step out for a few weeks or months at a time for reserve duty, guard duty, or even a deployment, and then come back with fresh insights on how to do our work better — or depart back to service with new ideas gleaned from how Anduril builds or puts its tech into practice. I’ve heard this described by colleagues as a flywheel of military learning. It definitely feels that way.

We also want to ensure that our policies reflect our commitment to service. Andurillians receive up to 30 days of fully paid military leave per 12-month period, with full benefits and equity vesting included. For leave periods beyond the 30-day duration, those who serve are eligible for up to two years of military pay differential, and a six months-long retention of all equity vesting and healthcare benefits. And since 2025, our full-time conversion rate of SkillBridge Fellows is over 90% and our retention of those converted Fellows is 98%.

Each of these policy decisions are nice things to have and they are measures that we take pride in — but more than any single perk accrued, they represent the value we put on attracting and retaining our nation’s finest.

And these numbers really only tell part of the story. What stands out to me when I walk our factory floors or watch a prototype test is the conviction. For Anduril’s veterans, the success or failure of a product in the field is personal. It is the difference between ensuring the safety of their fellow service member, who also took that same oath to wear the uniform. The design choices, welds, and software integrations we do every day are tangible commitments to defend our warfighters to our utmost ability. Anduril’s veterans won’t even entertain letting them down as an option.

The world is getting more dangerous. Our adversaries are growing in strength and sophistication. In response, the U.S. and its allies need a whole lot more: more interceptors to defend against enemy drones and missiles. More autonomy to keep our soldiers out of harm’s way. And more production, more factory floors, and more veterans who are ready to do their part — because Anduril is ready for you.

In the months ahead, we’ll be hiring for thousands more roles across the organization, especially those at the beachhead of our push toward mass manufacturing. Perhaps one of those roles is for you.

It’s time to Rebuild the Arsenal. This Veterans Day, Anduril honors your service by asking you not to join our mission, but for us to join yours.

Find your role at anduril.com/veteran-careers.

By Matthew Kaplan, Chief Administrative Officer at Anduril Industries
