Gate Ventures Weekly Crypto Recap (November 16, 2026)

TL;DR

  • U.S. equities posted broad-based gains for the week ending November 13, 2026, as early-week momentum offset Friday’s technology-sector weakness. The S&P 500 advanced 2.51% to 7,561.67, the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.27% to 25,136.27, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 2.07% to 52,347.55.
  • Crude oil prices extended their decline as easing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East reduced supply risk premiums. Brent crude settled at US$98.411 per barrel (-2.59%), while WTI crude closed at US$89.256 per barrel (-2.01%).
  • The benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yield rose 29 basis points to 5.15%, reversing five consecutive weeks of declines. Above-consensus October CPI inflation (+0.4% MoM), alongside hawkish Federal Reserve commentary, prompted markets to reprice expectations toward a higher probability of a December rate hike.
  • The AI and semiconductor sector experienced a sharp divergence in performance, with Nvidia declining 12.75% following reports of CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation, while AMD (+18.93%) and Broadcom (+18.16%) posted substantial gains. All eleven S&P 500 sectors finished the week higher, reflecting broad-based market strength.
  • Spot gold declined 4.57% to US$3,884.685 per ounce, as rising real yields and U.S. dollar strength weighed on precious metals. The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) advanced to a 19-month high, reinforcing pressure on non-yielding assets.
  • U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded an aggregate net holdings reduction of 17,279.54 BTC, primarily driven by declines in IBIT holdings. Meanwhile, spot Ethereum ETFs demonstrated relative resilience, registering a modest net holdings increase of 1,758.58 ETH.
  • DTCC outlined plans to expand its infrastructure capabilities to Solana, while wallet-level access restrictions highlight ongoing regulatory and compliance considerations.
  • Index Industries secured US$50 million in seed financing to develop a housing-linked investment index product, reflecting continued investor interest in alternative investment products.
  • Alta raised US$47.5 million in Series A financing to develop a compliance-focused Layer 1 blockchain, highlighting continued investment in blockchain infrastructure designed to address regulatory requirements.

Macro Overview

Equities Rebound Broadly as AI Leadership Reshuffles and Hot CPI Fuels December Rate-Hike Bets

Global financial markets staged a broad recovery during the week ending November 13, 2026, navigating an upside inflation surprise, a sharp hawkish repricing in interest rate futures, easing geopolitical oil risk, and major leadership upheaval in the artificial intelligence sector. On Tuesday, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that October headline Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 0.4% month-over-month, above consensus expectations of 0.3%, while Core CPI advanced 0.3% month-over-month against a 0.2% forecast, driven by sticky shelter (+0.3%), food (+0.5%), and transportation services (+0.8%) costs (with official 12-month comparisons omitted due to the 2025 federal shutdown data gap). Friday’s Producer Price Index (PPI) reinforced persistent pipeline pressure, rising 0.3% month-over-month and 6.5% year-over-year, its fastest annual pace since late 2022. Coupled with firm policy remarks from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller and New York Fed President John Williams, interest rate futures repriced the probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the December FOMC meeting to roughly 88%, propelling the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield up 29 basis points to 5.15%, its highest close since October 19. Meanwhile, crude oil continued its retreat as President Trump signaled a pullback from further military strikes on Iran and the International Energy Agency projected global oil supply returning to surplus, dragging Brent crude down 2.59% to US$ 98.411 per barrel and WTI crude down 2.01% to US$ 89.256 per barrel.

Equity market dynamics shifted from outright tech liquidation to sharp intra-sector dispersion and broad cyclical participation. Early in the week, easing energy prices fueled a strong relief rally across risk assets before governance turmoil struck the semiconductor space. On Thursday, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned after 33 years at the helm amid an independent board investigation, with CFO Colette Kress appointed interim CEO. Nvidia shares sank 12.75% across the week, bringing its drawdown from its October 6 peak to nearly 32%. Crucially, rather than triggering a wider AI sell-off, institutional capital rotated aggressively into competing chipmakers, infrastructure providers, and cash-rich mega-caps: AMD surged 18.93%, Broadcom jumped 18.16%, and neocloud operator CoreWeave rallied 24.7% on Wednesday following strong third-quarter earnings, while Apple reached a record close of US$ 356.27 (+4.1% on the week) and Tesla climbed 8.9%.

Unlike prior weeks dominated by narrow defensive positioning, market breadth expanded decisively, with all eleven S&P 500 sectors finishing in positive territory. Sector gains were led by Energy (+5.42%), Industrials (+3.76%), Communication Services (+3.36%), and Financials (+2.66%), while the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index gained 2.37% — closely tracking the cap-weighted benchmark. For the full week, the S&P 500 advanced 2.51% (+184.81 points) from 7,376.86 to close at 7,561.67, recording its strongest weekly performance since August. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite gained 2.27% (+556.84 points) from 24,579.43 to 25,136.27, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 2.07% (+1,061.82 points) from 51,285.73 to 52,347.55, and the small-cap Russell 2000 added 1.32% (+36.38 points) from 2,754.11 to finish at 2,790.49.(1)

Asset Class Highlights

U.S. Dollar Index (DXY)

The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) surged 1.10% week-over-week from 102.28 to close at 103.41, reaching its highest level since April 2, 2025, and logging its strongest weekly advance since mid-September. The greenback was propelled by widening U.S. rate differentials as hot October inflation data cemented market expectations for a December Federal Reserve rate hike. (2)

U.S. Treasury Yields

Sovereign bond yields jumped sharply across the curve, snapping a five-week streak of declines as markets priced in renewed Fed tightening: 10-Year Treasury Yield surged 29 basis points from 4.86% to settle at 5.15%, its highest close since October 19. The 30-Year Treasury Yield rose 21 basis points from 5.26% to 5.47%. (3)

Gold

Finished at US$ 3,884.685 per ounce, sliding 4.57% from the previous week’s US$ 4,070.590 close. Bullion broke back below the US$ 4,000 psychological threshold and touched its lowest intraday level in over a year on Thursday, weighed down by surging U.S. real yields, a 19-month high in the U.S. dollar, and reduced Middle East geopolitical risk premia. (4)

Crypto Markets Overview

1. Main Assets

Crypto markets faced another sharp sell-off this week, with BTC and ETH declining 12.6% and 13.6%, respectively. Selling pressure persisted across spot ETFs, with BTC ETFs recording $1.07B in net outflows and ETH ETFs seeing $398.9M in net outflows. (5)

Market sentiment deteriorated further, with the Fear & Greed Index plunging from 29 last week to 10, firmly back in Extreme Fear territory. (6)

2. Total Market Cap

The total crypto market capitalization declined 11.3% over the week, while the market cap excluding BTC and ETH fell 7.7%. Meanwhile, the broader altcoin market, excluding the top 10 cryptocurrencies, dropped 9.8%, reflecting continued weakness across smaller-cap tokens.

3. BTC/ETH ETF Performance

Over the past seven days, FBTC recorded the largest increase in Bitcoin holdings, adding 1,631.07 BTC (+0.93%). Meanwhile, IBIT posted the steepest decline, shedding 15,896.14 BTC (-1.96%), followed by BITO and GBTC, which reduced their holdings by 728.47 BTC (-3.35%) and 706.64 BTC (-0.57%), respectively.

Collectively, the Bitcoin ETFs shown recorded a net reduction of 17,279.54 BTC. (7)

Among Ethereum ETFs, ETHA registered the largest increase, adding 29,315.30 ETH (+0.84%), followed by FETH with an addition of 6,224.03 ETH (+1.15%). In contrast, ETHE recorded the largest decline, shedding 20,194.66 ETH (-2.69%), followed by ETH and ETHW, which reduced their holdings by 8,330.83 ETH (-0.94%) and 2,287.32 ETH (-2.09%), respectively.

Overall, the Ethereum ETFs shown posted a net increase of 1,758.58 ETH.(8)

4. Top 30 Crypto Assets Performance

Among the top 30 crypto assets, prices fell by an average of 9.6%.

The Key Crypto Highlights

1. DTCC plans Solana expansion as wallet rules limit access

The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation plans to bring its securities-tokenization service to Solana in the second half of 2027, allowing eligible DTC-held assets to be issued as digital tokens. However, US regulatory rules require holders to use registered wallets and restrict transfers to approved DTC participants, limiting access for retail investors and DeFi protocols. While the move could strengthen Solana’s institutional and real-world asset ecosystem, its impact will depend on adoption, liquidity and active use by financial institutions. (9)

2. Deutsche Bank launches institutional crypto custody in Europe

Deutsche Bank has launched a digital-asset custody service for corporate and institutional clients, initially supporting Bitcoin, Ether and selected stablecoins, including USDC, EURC and AllUnity’s EURAU. The bank will manage clients’ wallets, private keys and asset transfers under its EU MiCA registration, with Germany’s BaFin serving as regulator. Deutsche Bank is also exploring tokenized money-market funds, crypto exchange-traded products and stablecoins with Deutsche Asset Management, while planning expansion into Asian financial centres in 2027. (10)

3. South Korea to allow crypto withdrawal freezes over suspected hacks

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission plans to let crypto exchanges temporarily suspend deposits and withdrawals when a cyberattack is suspected, rather than requiring confirmation of an actual breach. The regulator will issue a no-action letter permitting preventive freezes under specified conditions, following recent security incidents involving GOPAX and Prabang. Exchanges will also face stricter requirements for round-the-clock monitoring, cold-wallet reserves, insurance coverage and incident response, while platforms without continuous monitoring must restrict transactions to staffed hours. (11)

Key Ventures Deals

1. Index Industries raises US$50M in seed funding to build a housing-linked investment product index

New York-based Index Industries emerged from stealth with US$50 million in seed funding led by Lightspeed Venture Partners, with participation from Polychain Capital, Galaxy Ventures, Wintermute Ventures and other institutional investors. The funding will support its technology and infrastructure for making broad exposure to the US housing market more accessible to investors.

Index Industries combines a daily home-price index, tokenization technology and capital-markets services to support housing-linked investment products. Its first benchmark, the US Home Price Index, will underpin USHP, a liquid product issued by USHP Limited and expected to launch on Ethereum in 2026 before expanding to Solana in 2027. The company was co-founded by former Polychain president Josh Rosenthal and Longmark CEO and former Blackstone executive Brian Hardecker. (12)

2. Alta raises US$47.5M in Series A funding to develop compliant L1 blockchain

Blockchain startup Alta raised US47.5 million in a Series A round led by Index Ventures, with participation from GV, Blackbird Ventures, Square Peg and Airwallex-backed Capital49. The round follows US32.5 million in seed funding primarily backed by global payments company Airwallex.

Formerly known as Metal, Alta is developing a Layer 1 blockchain with built-in privacy and compliance tools for banks and large companies processing stablecoin and other digital-asset transactions. Airwallex is also providing access to its global customer network as a distribution channel for the platform. Alta has 25 employees across San Francisco and Melbourne and was co-founded by Loong Wang and former Meta executive Catherine Porter. (13)

3. Marble raises US$11M in seed funding

Marble launched with US$11 million in seed funding led by Polychain Capital, with participation from Maven 11, Ripple, Susquehanna Crypto, Lattice, Blockwall and other strategic investors. The company plans to use the funding to expand its team and develop institutional trading and financing infrastructure for emerging markets spanning digital assets and artificial-intelligence compute.

Marble operates through two businesses: Marble Markets, an institutional platform for trading digital assets, real-world assets, prediction markets and compute contracts; and Marble Compute, which is developing private-credit financing for independent cloud providers and GPU operators. The company was founded by co-CEOs Matt Ballensweig, formerly global head of trading at BitGo, and Leon Marshall, formerly CEO of Galaxy Digital’s European business. (14)
