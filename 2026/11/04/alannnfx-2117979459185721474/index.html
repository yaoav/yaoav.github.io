Turning 𝕏 Posts Into Brand Deals

发表于2026-11-04|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @alannnfx（Alan） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

~55k followers, brand deals that reached $10k/month, and an inbox full of people asking for free stuff. How the inbound actually happens, plus the Grok Bot setups I’d copy to handle it. Every prompt is in here.

My brand deals on 𝕏 reached $10k/month.

I have ~55k followers. Pretty small next to a lot of creators.

The 𝕏 brand alone has made me multiple six figures.

The brand deals I’ve closed never started with a pitch from me. They started with someone who was already reading my posts and sent a DM.

Those DMs don’t arrive alone though.

This month I posted about getting an insane amount of DMs asking for free handouts.

Reviews, reposts, “nice win, can you send me some $$?”

The DM that turns into a paid deal sits in that same inbox, a few messages down.

So this guide has two parts:

how posting turns into brand deals and DMs
6 Grok Bots I’d set up so the good DMs don’t get buried

This is for you if

  • you post on 𝕏 and want brand deals or clients from it
  • you already get DMs and half of them are noise
  • a brand has sent you a brief and you had no idea what to charge

Part 1: How posts turn into inbound

1. People DM after they’ve been reading for a while

I posted for over a year before I really monetised anything.

Plenty of those posts got 4-5 likes.

When the deals did come, they came from people who had been reading for a long time. By the time we talked, the sale was pretty much made.

So when a brand pays for one post, they’re paying for everything you posted before it too.

These days people take $25 for a post.

I get why. But that $25 post stays on your profile, and the next brand that checks you out will scroll right past it.

2. People DM a person

Had Grok Bot audit my account. 113 posts from the last 6 weeks, split by topic.

crypto was about half of them and did 2.85% engagement
lifestyle posts did 5.11%

Crypto is a big part of my history on here and it still came out lowest.

When I posted that I’m back to growing 𝕏 accounts and want to help a few founders & creators build theirs, it got 73 replies.

It says what I do now and who it’s for, in four lines.

On July 30 I posted that it was a great day: a new warm lead for content management / 𝕏 growth and one creator inbound.

Two messages. Still a great day lol

3. Brands scroll back before they DM

Before a brand reaches out, they can scroll back and see:

what you sell
how often you sell it
whether every product gets the same glowing review

This is why I’m careful with cheap deals. Every one of them stays in that scroll.

Same with the paid partnership label.

In my experience it does very little damage to your credibility. If anything, it helps it.

Most people are still afraid to use it.

4. Make it obvious what a brand would pay you for

Work with brands is the most typical creator monetization plan.

Okay. Which brands?

What are they paying for?

Why is your audience useful to them?

I’d put the answers in plain words in your bio and pinned post. A brand manager scrolling your profile should get it in 10 seconds.

5. Know what you’re actually selling

Creator deal maths can get stupid very fast.

$1,000 sounds fine at first.

Until you find out the brand wants 12 months of usage, category exclusivity, three revisions + the post tomorrow.

All of a sudden you’re selling a lot more than one post.

Pricing a deal has to cover:

reading the brief
checking the product
protecting the wording
handling revisions
deciding whether the campaign fits your audience

6. Ask before you quote

When a brand DMs “what’s your rate?”, I’d answer with questions first.

what’s the product + who is it for?
how many posts, and when?
do you need usage rights or exclusivity?
what budget do you have in mind?

Their answers change the price more than your follower count does.

And brands that won’t answer any of these usually aren’t worth the back and forth.

Each bot gets one job.

Give it the job, the rules, and what it’s never allowed to do. Then let it do the boring part and keep the yes/no for yourself.

Hard rule for all six: nothing gets sent, accepted or agreed to until you read it.
