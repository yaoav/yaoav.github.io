"Next Time We'll Pay" Usually Means Never: @k2sbhai on AI, Brand Deals and Honest Reviews

发表于2026-11-22|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @k2sbhai（K2S） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Two years in web3 got @k2sbhai nothing. Then he moved to AI, started testing every new model himself, and turned it into real work with brands. We talked about the posts that blew up, his rules before recommending anything, how he picks brand deals, and what he’d tell a small creator who gets a “we’ll pay next time” DM

Q1. In May you posted: “2026 already and i still dont have 10k followers. its over for me yes or no?” Today you have over 10,000. So, was it over? Talk to me about what happened between that post and now.

No, it was not over. When I posted that, I was stuck and it felt like nothing was moving. I kept posting every day and moved fully into AI. My NVIDIA free models post in June did 605K views, and then on 5 October I hit 10K followers. Now I am at 16K+. It was not over, I just had not found my thing yet.

Q2. You named yourself after Sakata Gintoki from Gintama because, in your words, he’s “hardworking, funny, charismatic, and knows how to go with the flow.” Where does “going with the flow” show up in how you work today?

I don’t stick to one plan. When web3 stopped working, I moved to AI. When a new model drops, I test it and share it if it’s good. I just follow what is working and keep showing up. That is going with the flow for me.

Q3. In May you replied to someone, “I use ChatGPT only. Is claude good?” Five months later you’re running Opus vs Gemini tests and calling Opus “unbeatable.” Talk to me about that switch from crypto to AI. What made you go all in, and what was the first thing you built or tested that made you think “okay, this is my thing”?

I was in web3 for around 2 years, but at the start of 2026 everything went dead for me. AI was the opposite: there was something new every day. In April I used free Claude to build a small app for electronics shops to track installments, and I actually sold it. That is when I thought, okay, this is my thing. Then my AI posts started doing well and I went all in.

Q4. In June you wrote a post that really stayed with me: “just 6 years ago, my life was a mess, i relied on my parents for everything… now I send them money every month.” Can you talk to me about that change? What was the turning point for you?

6 years ago I was just playing games all day: PUBG, Free Fire, Fortnite, Minecraft, GTA 5 Online. The turning point came after 10th grade, when I decided to start a side hustle. Then web3 came. I gave it 2 years and worked with a lot of projects, but I got nothing out of it. At the start of 2026 I moved to AI, and now it’s actually working. Now I send money to my parents every month, and nothing beats that feeling.

Q5. Every creator has those days when it feels like nothing is working. You’ve been very open about the hard months too. What did your darkest stretch look like, and what kept you going?

The worst part was posting every day while nobody was watching. I applied to 100+ projects and got rejected. My first airdrop was worth over $10K, then they marked me ineligible. Then at the start of 2026 the whole web3 thing went dead for me. What kept me going was simple: I did not want to go back to doing nothing, so I kept showing up.

Q6. Back in May you wrote “imagine hating ai tools in 2026, you are never gonna make it like that… dont be anti ai, rather go with the flow.” Every day there are dozens of new tools. How do you decide what’s worth your attention? Basically, how do you work on your taste?

I test everything myself first. If the output is bad, I don’t post it, even if everyone is hyping it. I give the same prompt to 2 or 3 models and compare the results. That is how I build my taste.

Q7. Something I didn’t expect: you ran your own interview series, “Creators Journey,” asking other creators about their biggest W, their hardest phase and their advice for beginners. So let me turn your own questions back on you: what’s your biggest W so far?

Hitting 10K followers on 5 October. In April I was at 2,500. The bigger W is that brands pay me now and this is my real work. I also send my parents money every month, and that is a big W for me.

Q8. Your two biggest posts ever are both about getting top models for free: “$250 in Claude credits” with over 520K views, and “GPT-5.6 and claude for FREEEEE” with 256K. Both have more than 3,000 bookmarks. Why do you think “free access to the best model” hits so hard, and have you been able to reverse-engineer it?

Everyone wants the best model, but most people can’t pay $20+ every month, especially outside the US. So when you show them how to get it free, they save it right away. My format is simple: a short hook, what you get, steps I tested myself, and the catch if there is one.

Q9. Lately your strongest organic format is “uncensored” local AI: running open models offline on your phone or laptop. The Android guide alone got over 116K views. When I talk to people about this, I hear very different reasons: privacy, being tired of refusals, just curiosity. Why do you think people want it so much? And where is your personal line: what would you never post, even if you knew it would go viral?

Privacy, people being tired of refusals, and curiosity. People want to run their own AI on their own phone or laptop. My line is that I never post something I didn’t test, and I never hide the risk. If something can get your account banned, I say it clearly. I will never post anything that can hurt people, even if it would go viral.

Q10. When you share an app or a repo with tens of thousands of people, what’s your own checklist before you hit post? How do you decide something is safe enough to recommend?

I try it myself first. I check that the offer is real. I write simple steps and mention the catch, like an expiry, a card charge or a country limit. If it’s paid, I disclose it. If I’m not sure it’s safe, I don’t post it.

Q11. In September you shared your whole AI stack: Claude $20, Cursor Pro, OpenRouter, Obsidian, Gemini free for a year, total “$82/m.” If you could keep only one subscription, which one stays?

This one is tough, but I’d keep Grok Bot. It does my research, news and automations every day and saves me the most time.

Q12. Sometimes you post about a model before there’s even an official blog, like “Kimi K3.1 Pro is officially here, no official blog or no benchmarks yet.” What’s your discovery pipeline: OpenRouter, Hugging Face, GitHub trending, Discords, friends?

OpenRouter, OpenCode, Hugging Face and Arena. Models show up there before the official blog. I also hear things from other creators. But I always test it first, and if it’s not confirmed, I say so.

Q13. You wrote “the craziest part of 2026 is we’re using AI to write prompts for another AI.” Which part of your own work have you already handed over to AI, and which part do you think you’ll never automate?

Research, news tracking and first drafts go to AI, mostly Grok Bot. What I will never hand over is testing the product and deciding if it is safe to recommend. The final post also has to sound like me.

Q14. Your bio says “On my way to $10k/mo MRR.” For a lot of people reading this, that’s the dream. Can you talk to me about what that path looks like for an AI creator? What are the main income streams, and roughly where are you on the way?

For me it’s mostly brand deals with AI projects. Right now I make around 10% of that goal. I don’t have a SaaS yet. I want to build one, but I’m busy in real life right now, so I’ll build something when I get time.

Q15. Brand partnerships are a big part of the creator economy now, and you’ve been pretty clear on your principles. You once wrote that if you don’t disclose a paid partnership, “u are cooked.” How do you choose which brands to say yes to, and how do you keep a review honest when a brand is involved?

If it’s paid, I disclose it. I have rejected deals because they didn’t want the paid tag. I only say yes to things I can actually try, and if it’s bad, I don’t post it. My reputation matters more than one payment.

Q16. You told a story about a founder who asked you to promote his AI product for free and said “next time we’re gonna pay $10-15.” What would you tell a small creator who gets that DM tomorrow?

Say no politely. “Next time we’ll pay” usually means never. If their product is good, they can pay you now. Respect your own time and people will respect it too.

Q17. In June you wrote this might be “the beginning of the end for US AI dominance” and “It’s time for Chinese AI models to shine.” Almost six months later, were you right?

Half right. Chinese models like DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi and Qwen really did shine, and a lot of them are free or cheap. But US AI is not done. People still go crazy for Claude, GPT and Gemini. It’s a real race now, and users are winning.

Q18. You joked “my company in 2026 be like: CEO: Grok Bot, CTO: Grok Bot… Intern: Grok Bot.” Joke aside, what’s the first job you think agents will fully take over, and what’s your timeline?

Research and first drafts. Agents are already doing that: searching, summarizing and writing a first version. I think in 1 to 2 years most of that work will be done by agents, and humans will just check it.

Q19. You’ve joked a few times that “we are destined to be unemployed forever, thanks to Claude.” For someone reading this who’s genuinely scared about their job, what would be your advice?

Don’t fight AI, learn it. I had no fancy background, I just learned the tools and it changed my life. Use AI in the work you already do and get better at it. The people who learn it will be fine.

Q20. You once posted about “Ikigai”: “what do I love? what does the world need? what can I be paid for?” How would you answer those three questions for yourself today?

What I love is testing new AI tools and sharing them in a simple way. What the world needs is honest info, not hype. What I can be paid for is working with AI brands I actually trust
