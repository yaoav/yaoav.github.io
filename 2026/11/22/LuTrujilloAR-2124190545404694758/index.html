The ONLY REAL guide on how to increase LinkedIn Acceptance rate to +50% (based on 44,396 invites)

发表于2026-11-22|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @LuTrujilloAR（Luciano Trujillo） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Hey there. What I’m about to tell you here is, sincerely, the ONLY real article in the entire internet on how to improve your LinkedIn acceptance rate by A LOT and FAST.

If you search online or ask claude, chatGPT or grokbot how to increase your LinkedIn acceptance rate, you will get complete generic advice. Go ahead, try it.

Sure, having a better looking profile, keeping request natural and low pressure, are all thing that do make sense and I recommend doing, but by no means are what really moves the needle.

The problem are not the LLMs, but that all articles in the internet are generic bluff with no data to back up what they say. Even the most “trusted” sources.

In Kairon, we have analyzed more than 44k LinkedIn invites and I will give you the EXACT things that move the needle and BY HOW MUCH. Scientifically.

But you may be asking, is acceptance really that important? Absolutely. It’s one of the most important metric on LinkedIn outreach. If they don’t accept, you cannot even attempt to send a message (in free accounts).

LinkedIn outreach has a problem of volume. You can invite up to 30 people a day. So if your acceptance rate is low, you are gonna wait too much time to try to sell to people. You need to maximize acceptance rate as much as possible.

Ok Lucho enough yapping, lets get to it. Here is what really matters for improving acceptance rates:

1) Mutual Connections

This is, singlehandedly, THE most important factor. Never forget: LinkedIn is a people network. Not a database. A network. A people graph. That it’s most valuable asset.

People are tribe-driven. They connect with people they have something in common with. The more people you have in common, the more the receiver will feel you are part of their tribe.

As you can see, its an ever-increasing linear relationship. It just doesn’t stop improving!

So, if there is ONE thing you should change is this. When you build your lists of prospects to target, make sure you filter by 2nd degree and then make your agent sort by mutual connections so you start by the ones you have more mutuals with.

2) Recipient connections

Why does someone with more connections, even without having mutuals, accepts more than someone with less connections?

Well… Once you think about it, it’s pretty obvious. People with more connections, have gained those connections probably by being more prone to accept and send invites. People with low amount of connections probably don’t really value having a big network, or are not even paying attention to LinkedIn.

3) Recent activity

Recent activity shows how much they are inside LinkedIn. If they are posting, commenting or reacting to others posts, they WILL receive your invite and will evaluate if accepting or not. Compare that to someone who’s last post was a year ago, and doesn’t like anything. Probably they don’t even have their notifications on.

4) Amount of months in current role

People who are new to a role tend to be more open to LinkedIn connections because they’re actively building their professional network, establishing credibility, exploring opportunities, and learning about their industry. they have a stronger incentive to expand their network than someone who’s been in the same position for years.

5) Being a female

You make your own conclusions of this. I’m just stating facts.

But yeah, you may want to hire more women in your sales team. It’s 10+ acceptance improvement.

Conclusions

So there you have it. No “be authentic and personalize your note” garbage. Just data from 44k+ invites.

Let’s recap what REALLY moves the needle:

  1. Mutual connections. The king. The more mutuals, the better, and it never stops improving.
  2. Recipient connections. People who collect connections accept connections. Shocking, I know.
  3. Recent activity. If they’re not on LinkedIn, they’re not accepting your invite. Simple as that.
  4. Months in current role. New in the role = hungry to build a network.
  5. Being a female. Again, I’m just stating facts.

Notice something? None of these are about YOU. They’re all about WHO you invite. Most people obsess over their note, their headline, their profile picture… and then send invites to random people with zero mutuals who haven’t opened LinkedIn since 2022.

Remember, you only get ~30 invites a day. Every invite you waste on someone who will never accept is a sale you’ll never get to try.

So stop polishing your message and start picking better targets. Filter by 2nd degree, sort by mutuals, prioritize active people with big networks who just started a new role. That’s it. That’s the secret.

Your profile still matters, don’t get me wrong. But it’s hygiene more than a growth hack.

Now go build better lists. And if you don’t want to do all of this by hand, well… you know where to find us (heykairon.com)
