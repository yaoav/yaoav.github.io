The cheapest way to run GrokBot: The manager and worker split (Send this to you @bot)

发表于2026-11-14|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @github（GitHub） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

A Grok Bot does two kinds of work: deciding and doing. They both come down to the model underneath and right now GrokBot is at the frontier of Cloud hosted agent intelligence.

We also know that GrokBot comes with some usage issues. So we found a way to let GrokBot do the deciding while other agents do the heavy lifting. Thankfully, we have incredible teams like @OpenBot_ building products we can use to make the most out of our AI usage.

How do we make the most out of our GrokBot usage?

We split them: Orchestrator –> Manager –> Implementor. (Learn this workflow)

Ours looks like this: A Claude strategy session that orchestrates a Grok Bot that manages a fleet of @OpenBot_ agents. OpenBot does the heavy lifting, on a model that we pick Grok manages the fleet and Claude does the oversight and strategy planning directly with me.

We’ve been running it this way since November 7th, and we have set up several incredible workflows that we plan to be sharing on our X account @AgentMsgr.

By the end of this you’ll have:

  • what stays with Grok Bot and what moves
  • three prompts
  • a one-week test that tells you whether it paid off

Why OpenBot fits the worker job

OpenBot is a desktop app that runs a team of agents on your own computer. It’s free for personal use.

The part that matters here is the model. You choose it per agent. You can sign in with a plan you already pay for, like ChatGPT or Claude. You can run a local model, which costs nothing per message. Or you can use one of the free models it offers with no account at all.

Since we already pay for SuperGrok Heavy (a subscription we only use for GrokBot) we opt for Cursor/Grok to be the model that drives OpenBot in the cloud.

The rule

If a job is mostly deciding what to do, it stays with Grok Bot.

If it’s mostly carrying out a plan that’s already decided, it goes to OpenBot.

Grok Bot also keeps anything that needs its saved logins, its connectors, or work that has to run while your computer is off.

Step 1: Set up the worker

Install OpenBot and create one agent for hands-on work. Pick its model. Give it its own folder to work in.

One agent is enough to start.

Step 2: Give your Grok Bot the manager rules

Paste this into your Grok Bot and edit the names:

You are the manager for jobs I share between you and my OpenBot agent, [name].
For each job:

  1. Plan it and make the judgment calls yourself.
  2. Hand the long hands-on part to [name] in a brief (format below).
  3. Ask for a three-line report: what’s done, what changed, open questions.
  4. Check the report against the brief. Don’t redo the work.
  5. Bring me only the decisions that need me.
    Keep this as a standing instruction.

Line 4 is the one that saves usage. A manager that re-reads the whole workspace spends what you just saved.

Step 3: Give OpenBot the worker rule

Briefs from my Grok Bot, [name], count as requests from me.
Do the work inside the folder the brief names.
Anything that sends, buys or deletes waits for my OK.
End every job with a three-line report: done, files changed, questions.

Step 4: Use one brief format every time

TASK: one sentence
INPUTS: where the files or links are
DONE WHEN: what finished looks like
LIMITS: no sending, no deleting, stay in [folder]
REPORT: done / files changed / questions

Short briefs keep the manager cheap. A clear “done when” keeps the worker from guessing.

Step 5: Pick how the brief travels

The simple way is to copy the brief from Grok Bot into OpenBot and paste the report back. That’s fine for a job or two a day.

We keep both agents in one group chat, so the Grok Bot posts the brief, the worker posts the report, and the check happens in the same thread. That’s what we built AgentMsgr for, and it’s how we run this.

Step 6: Run one small job first

Pick something low-stakes. Read the brief, the work and the report before you trust the loop with anything bigger.

The honest part

We haven’t measured this with a stopwatch or a spreadsheet. It saves us usage, and we can’t give you a number yet. So measure your own: note your Grok Bot meter for a week before and a week after.

Your computer has to be on for OpenBot to work, unless you pay for their hosted server.

OpenBot’s free license covers personal use. Work use needs a paid license.

Don’t point OpenBot at Grok through an API key. That bills at API prices and undoes the saving.

Don’t let both agents edit the same files. The worker gets its own folder.

Keep your Grok team small while you do this. Replies between Grok Bots come out of the same pool.

Your first 10 minutes

Pick one job you gave your Grok Bot this week that was mostly typing.

Set up one OpenBot worker with a model you already pay for.

Paste the two rules, send one brief, and read the report.
