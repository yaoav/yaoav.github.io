How to Build a 24/7 AI Trading Desk With Grok Bot

发表于2026-11-07|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @rainbowda3h（Rainbow Dash） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Most people point AI at the market with one bot and one question

“Watch BTC and tell me what to do.”

That’s not a system. It’s a coin flip with a longer answer

A real desk splits the work. One person finds ideas. One reads charts. One tests. One says no. One executes. Nobody does two jobs, and nothing reaches the market without passing all of them.

Here’s how to build that desk in Grok Bot, run it on paper first, and keep one rule above everything: only one bot is allowed to place orders

Now here’s how it works.

Why one bot fails at trading

A single bot that finds the idea, grades it and executes it will always agree with itself.

It falls in love with its own story. A great headline on a broken chart still becomes a trade, because the same brain wrote both the headline summary and the buy order.

The fix is boring and old: separation of duties. Each bot has one job, its own tools, and the right to say no to the one before it.

The desk: five bots and a chief

Scout finds what is moving and why. Every 30 minutes in market hours it scans X, news and earnings for your watchlist, logs each finding with a source and a timestamp, and sends the top 5 forward. If nothing happened, it says “no signal” instead of forcing one.

Chart Analyst turns the story into a setup. Trend, key levels, volume against its average. Scores 1 to 10 and only passes 7 or higher. A great story on a broken chart is a no.

Strategist writes the exact plan: entry, stop, target, size. Then it backtests the same rules on its own computer. Reject the plan if there are fewer than 30 trades, if it fails on the most recent year, or if it only works with one exact setting.

Risk Officer is the only bot that can say no to everyone. It checks exposure, today’s loss, correlation with open positions and upcoming events. If a rule breaks, it names the rule and rejects. If not, it stamps APPROVED with a time.

Trader executes plans stamped APPROVED. Nothing else. Limit orders only, stop attached the moment it fills, never chases a price that moved too far.

Chief of Staff keeps the order of the route, settles disagreements using your rules, and sends you a morning brief and a close report. You talk to this one bot.

Step 1: write the Desk Rules first

Before you create a single bot, write the rulebook. Every bot reads it before acting.

This file is the whole desk. The bots are just people following it.

Step 2: one log everyone writes to

You can’t read a bot’s memory. So make every bot write down what it did.

Ask the Chief of Staff to create a Trade Journal sheet with these columns:

When something goes wrong, you can see which bot said what and why. If it’s not in the journal, it didn’t happen.

Step 3: give the desk eyes and hands

A team without tools is just a group chat.

Eyes: TradingView. It has an MCP server in beta, which lets your bots pull quotes, run the screener, read fundamentals and manage alerts. Tell the bot to add a custom MCP server and give it the URL from TradingView’s docs. You’ll need a paid TradingView plan.

Hands: a paper broker. Alpaca offers a free paper trading account and an MCP server. Create paper API keys and connect them the same way. Keep paper mode on.

Test both before trusting either. Ask for a screener table. Ask for your paper balance and open positions with the instruction “do not place any order.” If both come back clean, the desk can see and touch the market.

Setup details change, so check the current docs for both instead of trusting any copy-paste, including mine.

Step 4: lock the permissions

This is the step people skip.

A prompt is a request, not a lock. Bots are proactive by design.

Open each connection’s tool list and switch off everything a bot doesn’t need. Only the Trader gets order tools. Scout, Chart Analyst, Strategist and Risk Officer read only.

Step 5: routines

Once the desk works by hand, give it a schedule:

  • Scout: pre-market sweep, then every 30 minutes
  • Chart Analyst: morning scan, then hourly
  • Strategist: backtests the day’s new ideas every night
  • Risk Officer: before every order, plus a review before the close
  • Chief of Staff: morning brief, close report, a full review every Sunday

On a good day you read two messages and the desk did the rest. On a bad day it tells you which rule stopped it. Both are fine.

The kill switch lives outside the prompt

Tell the Risk Officer to trigger a kill switch if any of these happen:

  • daily loss hits 2%
  • 3 losing trades in a row
  • prices stop updating or look stale
  • any fill lands far from the planned price
  • any bot acts outside the Desk Rules

It cancels open orders, flattens positions, pauses the Trader and messages you.

Then set the same limits inside your broker account. Prompts can be argued with. Broker limits can’t.

Paper to real money: climb, don’t jump

Most AI trading setups die on the jump from paper to real money. Skipping rungs is how.

What this can’t do

Worth saying before you build it:

  • it is not high-frequency. Routines run minutes apart, not milliseconds
  • it can’t turn a weak idea into an edge
  • a good backtest is not proof, paper results have to match it first
  • usage is limited, and bot-to-bot messages count against it
  • you still make the final call

That list is not hedging. It’s the reason the other list is believable.

The actual shift

For years we asked AI to trade from a chat box. It was never the right shape.

A desk is a team, a process and a schedule that doesn’t break. Five bots with one job each, a chief that keeps order, a journal that remembers, and a risk officer whose no is final.

Build it once on paper. Let it prove itself. Then decide how much it earns your trust.

So the real question: is your AI still waiting for you to open a chat window, or is it already running a process while you sleep?
