We split our CI into 2x more jobs. That's why it got 4x faster.

发表于2026-11-03|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @opencolin（Colin Lowenberg） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Our goal was that every job in our pull request (PR) workflows finishes in about 60 seconds.

Our slowest TypeScript job took 226 seconds. Now every TS job finishes in under 60. Our slowest Go jobs got up to 5x faster.

How we did it was mostly boring: we took big jobs and split them into many small ones. TS went from 7 jobs per PR to 12. Go went from 17 to 27.

Splitting work across parallel jobs only helps if all those jobs can start at the same time. Not useful if you self-host your own runners (unless you have a ton of machines available) or use providers with concurrency or job start limits.

1. Split TS tests and stop re-testing main

The unit tests now run in 4 Vitest shards. The hard part was coverage. The coverage gate used to re-run the base branch’s tests on every PR. Now a merge job combines the coverage from all 4 shards and compares it against main’s stored summary. 226s → 48s.

2. Run Go integration packages in parallel, one database each

go test runs the packages in parallel, and each package gets its own clone of the test database. We also folded five separate suites into one run and made health checks poll every 2s. Slowest job: 216s → 111s.

3. Find the tests you run twice

The nodeagent and sandbox tests ran in two separate jobs. Now they run once, with -race turned on, split by test name across 8 jobs. The two old jobs took 210s and 269s.

4. Your test cache might be lying to you

A workflow compatibility test had been failing on main. Nobody noticed, because Go’s test cache kept returning an old passing result.

Why this breaks on capped CI

Every change above trades one long job for many short ones. That trade only pays off when nothing is rationing how many jobs can run at once.

  • Self-hosted fleet: if you own 10 machines, 27 jobs run in three waves, and you’ve just made your CI slower. You also pay for those machines when they sit idle at night.

  • Concurrency caps: if your provider runs at most N jobs at once, every extra split job waits for a free slot. You moved the time from “running” to “queued.”

  • Daily job limits: splitting 1 job into 8 uses up your quota 8 times faster.

  • Per-minute billing: every short job rounds up to a full minute, so 8 jobs of 50 seconds cost you 8 minutes.

We run on our own runners, billed per second. Same total compute, split into more pieces, with nothing waiting in line.

Give this to your agent to fix your CI and have it move your workflows to tenki.cloud.
