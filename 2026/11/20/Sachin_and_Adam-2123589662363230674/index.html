Learnings from our first 2M YouTube views

发表于2026-11-20|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @Sachin_and_Adam（Sachin and Adam） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

The Sachin and Adam show recently passed the 2 million view mark on YouTube.

We’ve had the privilege of profiling some of the most impactful companies in the world across biotech, energy, AI inference, quantum computing, robotics and more.

The show is at an exciting inflection point. The first 1 million views took 18 months. The next 1 million, just 3 months.

Below are 7 lessons from our journey so far.

1. Get started, then get good.

Our first documentaries look very different to our latest ones. We were still figuring out our voice and what works well on YouTube, given our roots in podcasting.

But through a combination of a) studying the greats, b) getting feedback from our community, and c) hiring the right talent, we’ve improved over time.

We still don’t get everything right, but we strongly believe that learning is downstream of doing.

2. Packaging is crucial

So much of YouTube comes down to packaging: title, thumbnail, hook (first 30 seconds). If no one clicks on your video, no one watches. If they click but leave immediately, the algorithm punishes you.

With that in mind, you need to do three things:

  1. Create a ‘curiosity gap’ (create a clear question or tension with your title/thumbnail)
  2. Confirm the click (immediately validate the viewer’s decision to click)
  3. Create open loops (keep teeing up questions you will answer later)

A few specific packaging learnings for our tech-focused channel:

  • Combine human subjects with impressive-looking tech
  • Lead with credibility by highlighting valuations ( “$10B startup”) or investors/competitors (NVIDIA, SpaceX, etc)
  • A/B test titles and thumbnails heavily - even small elements can have a huge impact

3. Social media is becoming interest media

Most of the content on your algo is not from people you follow. It’s based on what you’ve been recently interested in.

The direct implication of this is that you can no longer rely on huge follower counts as a proxy for how well your content is going to perform.

What matters is consistency: one format, one voice, one set of topics. For us, that’s the 10–20 minute documentary profiling frontier tech companies, with us as ‘curious outsiders’.

A lot of YouTube advice focuses on studying outliers and copying what they do. While this is undeniably powerful, you can’t rely purely on trend-hopping. Go deep on a niche, and your audience knows exactly what to expect, so they’ll watch almost regardless of the packaging.

4. Engaging with the community is important

Like any creator, we’ve opened ourselves up to online criticism. While some of the comments are noise, most are important.

For example, we’ve had thoughtful criticism on our adversarial framing of the ‘AI race’ with China and not probing enough on why a company’s approach is differentiated enough from competitors.

Our ultimate responsibility is to our audience, so we continue to prioritise nuance and transparency while still giving the companies the chance to tell their story in an exciting way.

5. Don’t neglect shortform

Shortform was something we didn’t really prioritise in the early days of the channel. But we’ve seen huge engagement recently.

In ~4 weeks, we’ve had ~700K views on our Instagram and ~140K views on YT Shorts, mainly from the low-hanging fruit of clipping up our long-form videos.

We were surprised by how clips from videos that didn’t perform as well on YouTube blew up on Instagram, opening us up to a much broader audience.

We’ve also learned the importance of going across platforms - LinkedIn, Insta, TikTok, and YouTube. Things that perform well on one platform may flop on others. It’s all about getting more shots on goal.

As we get the foundations right, our ambition is to do more short-form-specific content, in the vein of Cleo Abram explainers.

6. Talent is key

We hear it from founders all the time, but it’s worth repeating. Our recent success has been directly attributable to investing in the team, including hiring experienced longform videographers and editors.

7. Timeless topics win

The half-life of a YouTube video is nuts. We still get thousands of views a day from our long-tail of videos. Unexpectedly, videos can also suddenly pick up without us specifically pushing them. Check out the recent increase in slope on our PsiQuantum video:

It helps that we aren’t just jumping on trends, but profiling long-term, humanity-impacting stories. This ensures that our content stays evergreen.

These are early reflections, and our perspectives will certainly evolve as we continue to grow. You can continue to check out our work on YouTube, Insta and, of course, here on X.
