Meet Our Second Round of AI Node Grantees

发表于2026-07-15|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @yzhao010（Yue Zhao） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Brain-aligned generative models. Personal AI guardians. Secure LLM agent systems. AI for FDA regulatory documents. Epistemic memory for reasoning agents. We’re proud to announce our second round of AI Node grantees: researchers and builders working on the technical foundations of safe, private, and beneficial AI.

Private AI & Decentralization

Vora Aegis

If someone controls your personal AI, they can control you. @jesseposner from vora.io is building Aegis: a provably secure and loyal AI guardian for every person, one that manages and protects the digital life of the individual it’s dedicated to.

Project Sovereign

Trusted execution environments are not as secure as we assume, and AI increasingly runs on hardware no one can independently verify. @sudo_ml and Quintus Kilbourn are building secure, open, and verifiable silicon that lets users confirm a chip runs exactly as intended – no backdoors, no blind trust in manufacturers or cloud providers – reducing trust assumptions across AI coordination, health systems, safety research, BCIs, and critical infrastructure.

Fellowship for Safe AI Germany

Before 2026, Germany lacked a dedicated home for AI safety talent and research. Jessica Pu Wang founded Safe AI Germany (SAIGE), which has been running a three-month online incubator, and plans to establish a residential Berlin fellowship to further demonstrate the feasibility of SAIGE as a national AI safety organisation for Germany, building the talent capacity and institutional foundation the field needs.

Brain-Computer Interfaces & Neurotechnology

Brain-Aligned Generative Models

Generative AI has advanced rapidly, yet generative processes in the brain remain a blind spot for neuroscience. @AdrienDoerig from Freie Universität Berlin is building brain-aligned generative models that bring neuroscience and machine learning together – a path toward AI that is both more interpretable and more efficient.

AI Security & Interpretability

Adversarially Robust Safeguards for Generative Protein Design

AI can now design proteins that preserve the dangerous function of known toxins while looking nothing like them in sequence – and today’s biosecurity screening largely checks sequence similarity. @amiraliagz from @GeorgiaTech is developing more robust screening that flags risky designs by their structural and functional similarity, not sequence alone.

Audit-to-Patch Pipelines for Secure LLM Agent Systems

AI agents can fail in repeatable, high-impact ways that only surface after deployment. Yue Zhao from USC’s FORTIS Lab is building an audit-to-patch pipeline that detects and audits security risks in LLM agent code and configuration, then proposes safe, reviewable patches with automated checks – stopping safety failures before they ship and reducing regressions over time.

AI for Longevity Biotechnology

Hallucination-free generative AI for FDA regulatory documents

FDA submissions are required from the earliest stages of developing therapeutics, diagnostics, and devices – and they can demand hundreds of thousands of dollars, six months, and teams of experienced writers. Joel Shor from Nomos AI is building hallucination-free generative AI that helps junior scientists produce better regulatory documents at a fraction of the cost and time.

AI for Science & Epistemics

Epistemic Memory for AI Reasoning Agents

An AI reasoning agent is only as trustworthy as the memories it draws on. Florian Dietz is building an epistemic memory system for AI agents that tags every memory with its source and confidence, lets the agent trace where a claim came from and question it, and automatically swaps out unused memories while preserving the ones that matter.

**Congratulations to this second round of grantees. **The competition was rigorous, and we believe these projects have excellent potential.

Round 3 grantees to be announced soon.

Apply for our grants

Our AI Nodes fund AI-first projects across five focus areas: secure, private, and decentralized AI; interpretability; brain-computer interfaces; AI for science and epistemics; and AI for longevity biotechnology.

If your work fits, apply to join. Priority goes to researchers and builders who want to become active, in-person members of our San Francisco or Berlin hubs. Next deadline: July 31.

Want to support our grants program? Reach out at partnerships[at]foresight[dot]org
