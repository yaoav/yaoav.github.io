5 AI characters, 60 days, $91K, and the one SYSTEM under all of it

发表于2026-09-13|行业观察
|浏览量:

本文转载自 X 博主 @X（X） 的长文，原文链接，版权归原作者所有。

Generating a convincing AI video stopped being difficult a while ago. The hard part is making the same face stay the same face across hundreds of clips without anyone noticing the seams.

Over sixty days I stood up five fictional AI creators. No camera was switched on, no studio was booked, nobody was hired. The whole thing ran on five reference photos, three AI tools and a single content pipeline that does the repetitive work on its own.

Month 1: $34K. Month 2: $57K. Total: $91K.

Those are the figures the operation reported over that window. The revenue isn’t really the story though. The story is what happens after character one starts working: the second one costs a fraction of the effort, because the machinery underneath doesn’t change. New face, new personality, new niche, new audience, same backend.

The three-tool stack

Flux 1.1 Pro holds each character’s appearance steady from image to image. Kling 2.6 takes those stills and turns them into video, and it’s what decides how the movement reads. ElevenLabs gives each character her own voice, distinct from the other four.

None of that is a moat. The tools are available to anyone with a card. What isn’t available off the shelf is the wiring that makes the three of them behave like one system.

Three files keep each character herself

Every character is defined by the same trio of files.

face.json pins down the look: facial detail, hair, eyes, wardrobe, the environments she appears in. motion.md sets the physical behaviour, how she moves, how she reacts, what she does with her body on camera. calendar.md handles publishing, what goes out, to which platform, on what day.

That separation is why the five don’t blur together. One of them can be loud and fitness-driven, all motion and energy. Another can barely move at all, holding eye contact, posting lifestyle material. Put them side by side and they don’t read as one model wearing five different faces.

Building the pipeline

Week 1 goes into creating the characters and fine-tuning them. Week 2 is spent generating a library of movement and video. Week 3 is voices, one per character. Week 4 wires up distribution and fills the schedule. Week 5 automates the daily production loop.

After that it stops being a project and becomes a routine: generate the image, animate it, drop in voice where the clip needs it, resize, schedule.

Where most people get it wrong

They recycle the same movements until the pattern becomes obvious. They push identical content to every platform. They let the characters drift toward each other until two of them look and sound like sisters.

The other common mistake is shipping everything that comes out of the generator. Some clips are simply going to look machine-made, and those need deleting rather than posting. Consistency beats volume every time.

The actual opportunity

A single AI character is an experiment. Five of them, each with a different niche and a different audience, all running through one backend, is a different kind of thing entirely.

The technology stopped being the bottleneck. The edge now is taste: knowing what reads as human, what people will actually sit through, and how to keep a character recognisably herself month after month.

Once that first pipeline is running, character number six isn’t a new business.

It’s one more face plugged into the machine you already built.
